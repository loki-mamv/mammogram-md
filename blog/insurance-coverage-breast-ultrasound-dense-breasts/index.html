Does Insurance Cover a Breast Ultrasound for Dense Breasts?

November 2, 2026 6 min read By thrive.md Editorial Team
Medical advisor: Agustin Arrieta, MD — Otolaryngologist (ENT), South Florida

The short answer: Sometimes. Since plan years starting in 2026, a federal HRSA guideline requires most private and ACA plans to cover, without cost-sharing, extra imaging that is needed to complete screening. Routine ultrasound after a normal mammogram is not clearly included. Your state law, your plan type, and your doctor's order decide the bill.

This post covers the money side. If you need a refresher on what density means and which extra tests exist, read dense breasts explained first.

Does the ACA make a supplemental ultrasound free?

The Affordable Care Act requires most plans to cover HRSA's women's preventive services with no copay, coinsurance, or deductible. On December 30, 2024, HRSA published an updated breast cancer screening guideline in the Federal Register.

The new text says women "may require additional imaging to complete the screening process or to address findings on the initial screening mammography." It names MRI, ultrasound, and mammography, plus pathology evaluation. The notice says the change takes effect for most plans in 2026, for plan years that begin one year after the notice date.

Three limits matter:

  • Average risk only. The guideline covers women at average risk. It says recommendations for additional services for women at increased risk are "beyond the scope" of the guideline.
  • No separate density rule. HRSA declined requests to add dense-breast language. It said there are no randomized controlled trials to support separate recommendations for dense breasts.
  • "Indicated" is the key word. The imaging must be needed to complete screening. Whether a plan treats a routine ultrasound after a normal, dense mammogram as "indicated" is not settled in the text.

The U.S. Preventive Services Task Force also matters here. In its April 30, 2024 final recommendation, it gave biennial mammography from age 40 to 74 a grade B. For supplemental ultrasound or MRI in dense breasts, it issued an I statement: the evidence is insufficient to assess benefits and harms. An I statement does not trigger the ACA no-cost rule.

What do state laws require for dense breasts?

State law fills much of the gap. As of November 2, 2026, the DenseBreast-info.org state insurance map lists a supplemental screening coverage law in 39 states plus Washington, D.C. Eleven states show "no law": California, Hawaii, Kansas, Michigan, North Carolina, North Dakota, South Carolina, South Dakota, Utah, West Virginia, and Wyoming. These counts are our tally of the map's state entries.

Coverage is not the same as free. By our count of the map, about 29 states answer "no" to whether patients pay a copay or deductible for supplemental screening. Several of those have limits:

  • Alabama and Pennsylvania no-cost rules take effect January 1, 2027.
  • Florida's no-cost rule applies only to state employee plans.
  • Idaho covers one supplemental screening per year at no cost.
  • Arizona, Indiana, Ohio, and Rhode Island still allow cost-sharing (Arizona until 2027).
  • D.C., Maryland, Nebraska, New Hampshire, and New Jersey remove cost-sharing only in part.

The map also warns that state laws "don't apply to every policy." Self-funded employer plans, out-of-state plans, and federal programs such as Medicare, VHA, and TRICARE are outside state insurance law.

Does Medicare pay for a breast ultrasound?

Medicare has no no-cost supplemental screening benefit. Per Medicare.gov, Part B covers a screening mammogram once every 12 months for women 40 and older. You pay nothing if the provider accepts assignment.

For ultrasound, Medicare.gov says it "only covers medically necessary breast ultrasounds when your health care provider orders them." Diagnostic mammograms cost 20% of the Medicare-approved amount after the Part B deductible. Ask Medicare or your plan whether the same cost-sharing applies to your ultrasound before you book.

The HRSA guideline does not change this. It applies to private group and individual plans, not to Medicare. State laws do not reach Medicare either.

What about Medicaid and military coverage?

Medicaid rules differ by state, and no federal law now requires Medicaid to cover supplemental breast screening at no cost. The proposed Find It Early Act would amend the Medicaid benefit section of the Social Security Act to add it. Call your state Medicaid office or managed care plan to ask what applies to you.

TRICARE and VA care also sit outside state insurance laws, per the DenseBreast-info.org map. The same bill would add coverage in both programs.

Did the Find It Early Act pass?

No. As of November 2, 2026, the bill has not passed either chamber. The Senate version, S. 1410, was introduced April 10, 2025, and referred to the Committee on Health, Education, Labor, and Pensions. That is its latest action. The House version, H.R. 6182, was introduced November 20, 2025. It was referred to the Energy and Commerce Subcommittee on Health on January 15, 2026, and had 83 cosponsors.

The bill text would require no-cost screening and diagnostic imaging, including ultrasound and MRI, with no limit on frequency. It would apply to people with heterogeneously or extremely dense breasts, or at increased risk. It would cover private plans, Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, and VA care.

How do you check your coverage before the scan?

Use this checklist after your report says "heterogeneously dense" or "extremely dense." Each step ties to a rule above.

  1. Find your plan type. Ask HR or your insurer whether the plan is fully insured, self-funded, grandfathered, Medicare, or Medicaid. State laws skip self-funded and federal plans.
  2. Look up your state. Check your state on the DenseBreast-info.org map for coverage, cost-sharing, and effective dates.
  3. Get a written order. Medicare covers ultrasound only when a provider orders it. Most private plans also need an order.
  4. Ask for the billing codes. Ask the imaging center which procedure code and diagnosis code it will bill. Give both to your insurer.
  5. Ask the plan four questions. Is this covered as preventive or as diagnostic? Do I owe a deductible, copay, or coinsurance? Is prior authorization needed? Is the facility in network?
  6. Ask about the 2026 HRSA rule. If your plan is non-grandfathered, ask whether it treats your ultrasound as imaging needed "to complete the screening process."
  7. Get the answer in writing. Write down the representative's name, the date, and a reference number.
  8. Appeal a wrong bill. If you are billed despite a state no-cost law, ask the plan to reprocess the claim and cite your state law.

If your plan denies the scan, compare cash prices. Our guide to mammogram cost without insurance lists free and low-cost programs. If your doctor suggests MRI instead, mri.md explains how MRI scans work and how to prepare.

The bottom line

A breast ultrasound for dense breasts may be free, partly covered, or fully billed to you. Since 2026, the HRSA rule removes cost-sharing for imaging that is needed to complete screening in most private and ACA plans. State laws in 39 states plus D.C. add more coverage, but only for state-regulated plans. Medicare covers ultrasound only when ordered and medically necessary. The Find It Early Act, which would close these gaps, has not passed. Check your plan type and state law before the scan.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is a breast ultrasound for dense breasts considered screening or diagnostic?

It depends on why it is ordered. An ultrasound after a normal mammogram, done only because of density, is supplemental screening. An ultrasound to check a finding is diagnostic. Plans often apply different cost-sharing to each, so ask how your claim will be coded.

Does a high-deductible plan have to pay for my supplemental ultrasound?

Plans subject to the ACA must cover HRSA-listed preventive services without cost-sharing, and the 2026 HRSA update includes imaging needed to complete screening. Whether a routine density ultrasound qualifies is up to plan interpretation. Some state laws also require no-cost supplemental screening. Call your plan and ask before the scan.

Why does my employer plan ignore my state's dense breast law?

Self-funded employer plans are regulated under federal law, not state insurance law. DenseBreast-info.org notes that state laws do not apply to every policy, and self-funded plans are a main exception. Ask HR whether your plan is self-funded.

Does Medicare pay for a breast MRI if I have dense breasts?

Medicare.gov does not list a supplemental screening benefit for dense breasts. Medicare covers breast imaging beyond the yearly screening mammogram when a provider orders it as medically necessary. Part B cost-sharing usually applies, so confirm your share before the scan.

Will the Find It Early Act make supplemental screening free?

If it passes, it would require no-cost screening and diagnostic breast imaging for people with heterogeneously or extremely dense breasts. It would cover private plans, Medicare, Medicaid, TRICARE, and VA care. As of November 2026 it has not passed either chamber of Congress.
